The glue on an iron-on patch softens when it's heated, so the same heat that put it on will take it off. The patch comes away fairly easily; the leftover glue is the fiddly part. On most cotton and denim you can get it all off with an iron, some patience and a little solvent. Expect a faint outline on fabric that has faded around the patch, especially dark denim, as the fabric underneath will be a little darker.

Before you start, check the care label. If the fabric can't take a hot iron, try the freezing method instead.

## Method 1: heat (best for cotton and denim)

1. **Turn the garment inside out** and lay it flat on an ironing board, with the patch side down.
2. **Cover the back of the patch area with a cloth** and press with a hot, dry iron for 20 to 30 seconds.
3. **Turn it the right way round** and start lifting a corner of the patch with your fingernail, a blunt butter knife or tweezers.
4. **Peel slowly, reheating as you go.** If it resists, press again for 10 to 15 seconds. Warm glue lets go; cool glue tears the fabric.
5. **Peel at a low angle**, pulling back over the patch rather than straight up.

A hair dryer on its hottest setting also works for small patches or heat-sensitive fabric, though it's slower.

## Method 2: freezing (for delicate fabrics)

1. Put the garment in a plastic bag and freeze it for a few hours.
2. Take it out and immediately work at the patch edge with a fingernail. The cold makes the glue brittle, so it can crack away rather than stretch.

This works better on thinner patches and fabrics than on heavy denim.

## Getting rid of the glue residue

Some glue nearly always stays behind. Try these in order, from gentlest to strongest:

1. **Re-heat and blot.** Put a sheet of baking paper (or a paper towel) over the glue and press with a warm iron. Some glue lifts onto the paper. Repeat with fresh paper.
2. **Rub with a little washing-up liquid and warm water**, then scrub gently with an old toothbrush.
3. **Rubbing alcohol (isopropyl alcohol) or acetone-free nail polish remover** on a cotton pad. Test on an inside seam first, then dab the glue, wait a minute, and scrape gently.
4. **A [citrus-based adhesive remover](amazon:sticky stuff remover)**, used as the label says and tested first on a hidden area.

Wash the garment afterwards to remove any solvent.

| Method | Best for | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| Iron and baking paper | Cotton, denim | Gentlest; takes several passes |
| Washing-up liquid and toothbrush | Light residue | Safe on most fabrics |
| Rubbing alcohol | Stubborn glue | Test first; can fade some dyes |
| Adhesive remover | Heavy residue | Follow the label; wash afterwards |

## Don't

- **Don't use acetone on synthetics or acetate.** It can dissolve the fabric.
- **Don't yank a cold patch.** It can pull threads or leave a hole.
- **Don't put the garment in the dryer** while there's still glue on it; heat can spread it.

## Common questions

### Can you remove an iron-on patch and reuse it?
Sometimes. The glue on the back is usually spent, so sew it on next time, or use a [fabric glue](/guides/best-fabric-glue-for-patches/).

### Will removing a patch leave a mark?
Often a faint outline, because the fabric under the patch hasn't faded like the rest. It usually blends in over a few washes, and you can always cover it with a new patch.

### How do you remove a sewn-on patch?
Use a seam ripper or small scissors to cut the stitches from the inside, then pull out the loose threads with tweezers.

## The short version

Heat the patch from the inside with a hot iron, then peel it slowly while it's warm, reheating as needed. Lift leftover glue with an iron and baking paper, then washing-up liquid or rubbing alcohol, testing anything stronger on a hidden seam first.
