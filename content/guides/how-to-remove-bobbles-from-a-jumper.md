Bobbles, or pilling, are little balls of short fibres that work loose and tangle on the surface of knitwear where it rubs: under the arms, at the cuffs and where a bag strap sits. You can remove them with an electric fabric shaver, a de-pilling comb or a disposable razor, working gently on a flat surface. To slow them coming back, wash knitwear inside out on a wool or hand-wash cycle and dry it flat.

## Check the jumper first

- **The care label and fibre.** Wool, cashmere, acrylic and blends all pill. Acrylic and polyester bobbles are tougher and cling more, because the fibres are strong and don't break off.
- **How fine the knit is.** Fine cashmere and merino need the lightest touch. Chunky knits and very open weaves can snag in a shaver.
- **Holes or thin patches.** Shaving won't fix a worn-through elbow. Darn that first; see [how to darn a hole in a jumper](/guides/how-to-darn-a-hole-in-a-jumper/).

## What you need

- **A fabric shaver** (also called a de-bobbler or lint remover). A [battery or rechargeable fabric shaver](amazon:fabric shaver jumper de-bobbler) is the quickest option for most knitwear.
- **Or a de-pilling comb** or cashmere comb, for fine wool and cashmere. [Cashmere combs](amazon:cashmere comb de-pilling) are gentle and cheap.
- **Or a new disposable razor**, as a stop-gap.
- **A flat surface**, such as a table or ironing board, and a lint roller to tidy up.

## How to remove bobbles with a fabric shaver

1. **Lay the jumper flat** on a table or ironing board, smoothing it out so there are no folds. Never shave it while you're wearing it.
2. **Check the shaver's guard** is on and the blades are clean. If it has height settings, start with the guard furthest from the fabric.
3. **Hold the fabric taut** with your free hand around the area you're working on.
4. **Glide the shaver lightly** over the bobbles in small circles. Don't press down: let the guard skim the surface.
5. **Empty the lint chamber** often. A full chamber cuts badly.
6. **Turn the jumper over** and do the other side, then the sleeves, with your hand inside to keep them flat.
7. **Finish with a lint roller** to pick up loose fluff.

## How to use a comb or razor

**De-pilling comb:** brush in short, light strokes in one direction along the knit. It lifts and pulls the bobbles away. This is the gentlest method and the best for cashmere.

**Disposable razor:** use a new, dry razor. Lay the jumper flat, hold the fabric taut and shave lightly downwards with short strokes, as if shaving skin. Go carefully, because a razor can cut the knit if you press or catch a seam.

## How to stop bobbles coming back

- **Wash inside out**, on a wool or hand-wash cycle, at the temperature on the care label.
- **Wash knitwear with other knitwear**, not with jeans and towels that rub it.
- **Use a mesh laundry bag** for fine pieces.
- **Skip the tumble dryer.** Dry flat on a towel or a drying rack.
- **Wash less.** Air jumpers between wears; a quick shave every so often is easier than repairing felting.
- **Watch for friction points** such as bag straps, seatbelts and desk edges.

## Don't

- **Don't pull bobbles off by hand.** It drags out more fibre and makes thin spots.
- **Don't press hard** with a shaver or razor.
- **Don't shave over seams, buttons or embroidery.**
- **Don't shave a jumper while wearing it.**

## Common questions

### Does a fabric shaver damage jumpers?
Used gently on a flat jumper, it only cuts the loose bobbles on the surface. Pressing hard, catching a fold or shaving very fine knits too often can thin the fabric, so go lightly and stop once it looks smooth.

### Why do new jumpers bobble so quickly?
Short fibres and loose knits pill most, and some new knitwear has loose fibres on the surface that shed in the first few washes. It often settles down after the first couple of de-bobblings.

### Can you remove bobbles from a coat or sofa?
Yes. A fabric shaver works on wool coats, upholstery and blankets. Test on a hidden spot first, and keep the guard at its highest setting on thick fabrics.

### Does fabric conditioner stop bobbles?
It can reduce friction a little on some fabrics, but it isn't recommended for wool or for some technical fabrics. Gentle washing and drying flat make the biggest difference.

## The short version

Lay the jumper flat and glide a fabric shaver lightly over the bobbles, or use a cashmere comb on fine knits. Then wash it inside out on a wool cycle with other knitwear and dry it flat, so fewer bobbles form next time.
