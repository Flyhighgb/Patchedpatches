For a few patches, the household iron you already own is fine: the cotton setting, steam off and firm pressure do the job. A dedicated tool starts to pay off when you're applying a lot of patches, working on awkward spots like caps, sleeves and shoes, or selling patched items and need consistent results.

## What to look for

- **Even heat across the plate.** Household irons have steam holes and hot spots; heat presses are designed to heat evenly.
- **Adjustable temperature.** Delicate fabrics and thicker patches need different settings.
- **Pressure.** Iron-on glue needs firm, even pressure. Bigger presses do the pressing for you.
- **Size.** Match the plate size to your patches and the places you'll be putting them.
- **Safety.** Look for auto shut-off and a safety base or stand.

## How to use a heat press for patches

1. **Check the patch maker's instructions** for temperature and time. If there are none, start around the cotton setting on a household iron and adjust.
2. **Pre-press the fabric** for a few seconds to remove moisture and creases.
3. **Place the patch**, cover it with a Teflon sheet or baking paper, and press with firm, even pressure for the recommended time.
4. **Turn the garment inside out and press again** from the back.
5. **Let it cool fully**, then check the edges.

## What to avoid

- **Steam.** Turn it off or use a dry iron.
- **Pressing PVC or rubber patches.** They melt. Sew them on instead.
- **Leaving a hot press unattended**, and letting children use one without supervision.
- **Buying big before you need to.** A clamshell press only makes sense if you're doing batches.

## Common questions

### Do you need a heat press for iron-on patches?
No. A household iron with steam off, a pressing cloth and firm pressure works for most patches. A press gives more even, repeatable results if you do a lot of them.

### What temperature should a heat press be for patches?
Follow the patch maker's instructions, as glues vary. If there are none, start with a setting suitable for cotton and adjust for the fabric, and do a test on scrap fabric if you can.

### What's the best tool for putting a patch on a cap?
A mini iron, a small handheld press, or a hat press. On a structured cap, fabric glue is often easier. See our guide to the [best fabric glue for patches](/guides/best-fabric-glue-for-patches/).

## The short version

Your household iron is enough for a few patches. A mini iron is the cheapest upgrade for caps and tight spots, a handheld press gives even heat for regular crafting, and a clamshell press is only worth it for batches. Whatever you use: no steam, firm pressure, press from both sides.
