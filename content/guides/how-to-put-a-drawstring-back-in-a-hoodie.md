To put a drawstring back in a hoodie or joggers, fix a safety pin to one end of the cord, push the pin into one eyelet and work it through the channel by bunching the fabric up over the pin and smoothing it out behind, until it comes out of the other eyelet. No safety pin? A drinking straw, a hair grip or a bodkin all work too. It takes about five minutes, and a few stitches at the centre back stop it pulling out again.

## Before you start

- **Find the channel.** The hood or waistband has a tunnel of fabric (the casing) with a hole or metal eyelet at each end. On joggers the eyelets are usually at the front of the waistband, sometimes on the inside.
- **Check the cord ends.** If the plastic or metal tips (aglets) have come off and the cord is frayed, wrap the end tightly with sticky tape first, or it will unravel inside the channel.
- **Look for a seam inside the channel.** Hoods often have a seam at the centre back, where the pin can catch. Go slowly over it.

## What you need

- **A large safety pin** (about 4 to 5 cm). Bigger is easier to feel through the fabric.
- **Or a bodkin**, a blunt threading tool made for exactly this. A [bodkin or elastic threader](amazon:bodkin threader elastic drawstring) is cheap and useful for every drawstring and elastic job.
- **Or a drinking straw**, a hair grip or a chopstick for short channels.
- **Sticky tape** for frayed ends.
- **A replacement cord** if the old one is lost. [Hoodie drawstrings](amazon:hoodie drawstring cord replacement) come in standard lengths; measure the old channel plus about 30 cm (12 in) for the ends.

## How to thread it with a safety pin

1. **Pin the cord.** Push the safety pin through the end of the cord, close to the tip, and close it.
2. **Push it into one eyelet**, closed end first.
3. **Bunch and smooth.** Pinch the pin through the fabric, push the fabric back over it so it gathers up, then hold the pin and smooth the gathered fabric out behind it. Repeat, a few centimetres at a time.
4. **Keep hold of the tail.** Don't let the other end of the cord vanish into the eyelet. Tie a knot in it or clip it to the outside with a peg.
5. **Ease past seams.** If the pin sticks at a seam, wiggle it slightly and push it through flat rather than forcing it.
6. **Bring it out** of the other eyelet, take the pin off and even up the two ends.

## The straw method

For a hood, a large drinking straw is often quicker.

1. **Push the cord end into the straw**, or thread it through and fold it over, then tape it.
2. **Slide the straw into the eyelet** and push it through the channel, bunching the fabric up over it.
3. **Pull the cord through** when the straw comes out the other side.

This works best with wide, smooth cords. Thin round cords may slip out of the straw, so tape them in.

## Methods compared

| Method | Best for | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| Safety pin | Any hoodie or joggers | Always works; can open inside the channel if it's small |
| Bodkin | Regular use, long waistbands | Made for the job, won't open or snag |
| Drinking straw | Hoods with wide channels | Quick; tape thin cords in |
| Hair grip | Short channels | Grips flat cords; can slip on round ones |
| Chopstick | Wide hoods | Rigid, so struggles round curves |

## How to stop it pulling out again

- **Stitch it at the centre back.** With the cord even, find the middle of the hood or the centre back of the waistband and sew a few stitches straight through the casing and the cord. It can't slide out after that.
- **Knot the ends** with a simple overhand knot, bigger than the eyelet.
- **Replace lost tips.** Wrap the ends tightly with thread and dab on a little clear nail varnish or fabric glue, or fit [replacement cord ends](amazon:drawstring cord ends stoppers).

## Don't

- **Don't pull the cord through** without anchoring the other end.
- **Don't use a tiny safety pin.** It opens easily and is hard to feel.
- **Don't force it past a seam.** You'll tear the casing.
- **Don't wash the hoodie** with a loose drawstring before you've knotted or stitched it.

## Common questions

### How do you put a drawstring back in without a safety pin?
Use a drinking straw, a hair grip, a bodkin, or tape the cord to a chopstick. All work the same way: push the tool through the channel, bunching the fabric over it, and pull the cord out at the other end.

### What if the safety pin opens inside the hood?
Feel for the point through the fabric, close it from the outside if you can, and gently ease it back out the way it came. Use a bigger pin or a bodkin next time.

### Why does my hoodie string keep coming out?
Usually one end has been pulled while the other was loose. Stitching the cord to the casing at the centre back, or knotting both ends, stops it.

### Can I use any cord as a replacement?
Yes, as long as it's soft, not too stiff and fits the eyelets. Flat cords lie neater in hoods; round cords suit joggers. Seal the ends so they don't fray.

## The short version

Pin a large safety pin to the end of the cord, push it into one eyelet and work it through by bunching and smoothing the fabric until it comes out the other side. Then stitch the cord at the centre back so it can't pull out again.
