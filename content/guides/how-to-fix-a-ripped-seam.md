A ripped seam is one of the easiest mends there is, because the fabric itself isn't damaged: the stitching has simply come undone. Turn the garment inside out and backstitch along the original seam line, or, if you can only reach it from the outside, close it with a ladder stitch, which hides inside the fold. Both take 10 to 20 minutes and last as long as the rest of the garment.

## Check the seam first

- **Is it the stitching or the fabric?** If the threads have broken but both edges of fabric are intact, it's a seam repair. If the fabric itself has torn or frayed away from the seam, you'll need a patch or a darn as well.
- **Can you reach the inside?** Trouser crotches, side seams and sleeves usually can be turned inside out. Lined coats and stuffed toys often can't, so use ladder stitch from the outside.
- **Is it stretchy?** Jersey, knitwear and leggings need a stitch that stretches, or the repair will snap again.

## What you need

- **All-purpose polyester thread** in a matching colour. It's stronger and slightly stretchier than cotton.
- **A hand-sewing needle**: a fine sharp for woven fabrics, a ballpoint needle for jersey.
- **Pins or clips**, scissors and an iron.
- A [basic sewing kit](amazon:sewing kit for beginners) covers all of this.

## How to backstitch a seam from the inside

1. **Turn the garment inside out** and press the seam allowances flat so you can see the old stitch line.
2. **Pin the two edges together** along the original line, with the right sides of the fabric facing each other.
3. **Thread a single strand** about 50 cm (20 in) long and knot the end.
4. **Start 1 to 2 cm before the gap**, in the stitching that's still intact, so the new and old stitches overlap.
5. **Backstitch along the line.** Take a stitch forward, then bring the needle back down at the end of the last stitch and up again one stitch ahead. Keep stitches small, about 2 to 3 mm.
6. **Carry on 1 to 2 cm past the gap**, into the good stitching.
7. **Finish off** with three small stitches on top of each other, then trim the thread.
8. **Turn the garment right side out** and press the seam.

## How to ladder stitch a seam from the outside

Ladder stitch (also called slip stitch or invisible stitch) closes a gap where both edges are folded under.

1. **Fold the seam allowances inwards** along the old fold lines so the two edges meet.
2. **Bring the needle up** from inside the fold on one side, at the start of the gap, so the knot is hidden.
3. **Cross straight over** to the other fold and take a small stitch, about 3 mm, along inside that fold.
4. **Cross straight back** and take the same small stitch along the first fold.
5. **Repeat every few stitches, then pull the thread gently.** The stitches disappear and the edges close up like a zip.
6. **Finish off** inside the fold with a couple of tiny stitches, and bury the tail by pushing the needle through the fabric and out a few centimetres away before trimming.

## Which stitch to use

| Seam | Best stitch | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Trouser crotch or side seam | Backstitch, inside | Strongest hand stitch, takes a lot of strain |
| Shirt or dress side seam | Backstitch, inside | Neat and strong |
| Coat lining | Ladder stitch | Can't reach the inside easily |
| Cushion or soft toy | Ladder stitch | Invisible from the outside |
| Jersey, T-shirt or leggings | Small backstitch, slightly loose | Leaves enough give to stretch |
| Knitted jumper | Mattress stitch with yarn | Matches the knit and stretches |

## Strengthening a seam that keeps splitting

Seams that go in the same place again and again, such as the inner thigh of jeans, usually mean the fabric beside them has worn thin. Backstitch a second line just inside the first, a few millimetres away, and consider ironing or sewing a patch on the inside behind the worn area. See [how to patch a hole in jeans](/guides/how-to-patch-a-hole-in-jeans/).

## Don't

- **Don't use a running stitch** on a seam that takes strain. It pulls apart.
- **Don't pull stretch seams tight.** Leave a little slack or the thread snaps.
- **Don't stitch only across the gap.** Overlap the old stitching at both ends.
- **Don't use heavy thread on fine fabric.** It can cut through it.

## Common questions

### Is hand stitching as strong as a sewing machine?
A small, even backstitch is very nearly as strong as a machine straight stitch, which is why it's the standard hand method for seams. Running stitch is much weaker.

### Can I use fabric glue or hemming tape on a seam?
Hemming tape is meant for hems, not seams under strain. It can work for a quick fix on a lining, but it won't hold a trouser seam for long. Stitching is better.

### What colour thread should I use?
Match the fabric as closely as you can. If you can't, go slightly darker, which shows less than lighter thread.

### How do I fix a seam in a knitted jumper?
Use yarn rather than thread, and mattress stitch, which picks up the bars between knitted stitches on each side. It stretches with the knit.

## The short version

From the inside, backstitch along the old seam line, starting and ending in the stitching that's still good. From the outside, close the gap with ladder stitch, which hides in the folds. Keep stitches small, leave some give on stretchy fabrics, and press it afterwards.
