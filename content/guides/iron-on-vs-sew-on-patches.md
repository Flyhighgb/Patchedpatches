The quick answer: **iron-on is quicker, sew-on lasts longer.** Iron-on patches are great for cotton and denim that you don't wash every week. Sewing is better for anything that's washed often, gets heavy wear, or is made from fabric that can't take an iron. Many people do both: iron it on to hold it in place, then add a few stitches.

The backing matters more than people think, so it's worth knowing the options before you buy.

## Patch types by material

The front of the patch matters as well as the back:

- **Embroidered:** the classic raised thread patch. Bold, textured and hard-wearing. Fine detail and small text can be hard to stitch.
- **Woven:** thinner threads woven flat, which allows finer detail and small text. Flatter and smoother than embroidered.
- **Printed (dye-sublimation):** a printed design on fabric. Best for photos and gradients; colours can fade a little over many washes.
- **PVC or rubber:** moulded, waterproof and very durable. Popular on outdoor and tactical gear. **Never iron these**; sew them on or use hook and loop.
- **Chenille:** fluffy, looped yarn, the "varsity jacket" look. Sew on for best results.
- **Leather or faux leather:** often seen on jeans and hats. Sew on.

## Which to choose

1. **Is the fabric heat-safe?** Check the iron symbol on the care label. Three or two dots: iron-on is an option. One dot or crossed out: sew, use hook and loop, or glue.
2. **How often will it be washed?** Weekly washing favours sewing. A jacket washed a few times a year is fine with iron-on alone.
3. **Do you want to swap patches?** Hook and loop is the only practical way.
4. **Is it a hat, a shoe or somewhere awkward?** A fabric glue or a mini iron can be easier than a needle.

## Don't

- **Don't buy iron-on patches for a nylon or polyester jacket** expecting them to iron on; they'll need sewing anyway.
- **Don't trust peel-and-stick patches** in the washing machine.

## Common questions

### Are iron-on patches permanent?
They're semi-permanent. Applied properly they can last years, but hot washes and tumble drying weaken the glue. Add a few stitches for a truly permanent patch.

### Can you sew on an iron-on patch?
Yes. The glue backing doesn't stop you sewing through it, though thick backing can be stiffer to stitch. Ironing first and then sewing is the most secure option.

### Can you iron on a sew-on patch?
Not by itself, because it has no glue. You can add an iron-on adhesive sheet such as [Bondaweb](amazon:vlieseline bondaweb) to the back, or use a fabric glue.

### Which patches last longest?
Embroidered or PVC patches that are sewn on. Embroidery thread is hard-wearing and stitching doesn't weaken with heat the way glue does.

## The short version

Iron-on is quick and works well on cotton and denim. Sew-on is the most permanent and works on any fabric. Hook and loop is for patches you want to swap. If the fabric can't take an iron, or it's washed often, sew.
