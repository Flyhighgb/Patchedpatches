A belt loop usually rips off at the top, where it's folded under and bar-tacked to the waistband. To fix it, fold the raw end under, hold it in place with a clip, and sew it back down with strong thread and a dense row of back-and-forth stitches (a hand bar tack) across the loop. If the loop has torn a hole in the waistband as well, put a small patch behind it first.

## Check the damage

- **Loop only.** The stitches have broken but the waistband is fine. This is a quick 10-minute fix.
- **Loop torn and frayed.** The end of the loop itself has frayed. Trim it neatly and fold under a little more.
- **Waistband torn.** The loop has pulled a hole in the jeans. Patch that from the inside first, or the loop will tear straight out again.
- **Loop missing.** Make a new one from a strip of denim from an old pair of jeans or the hem of the leg if they've been taken up.

## What you need

- **Strong thread**, such as jeans or upholstery thread, or a doubled strand of polyester all-purpose thread. [Jeans thread](amazon:jeans thread denim heavy duty) blends in with the topstitching.
- **A strong, sharp needle.** A thimble helps, because you'll be pushing through several layers of denim.
- **Clips or a clothes peg** to hold the loop. Pins struggle with thick denim.
- **A small piece of denim or iron-on patch** if the waistband is torn.

## How to sew the belt loop back on

1. **Trim the end** of the loop so it's tidy, and pull out any broken threads.
2. **Fold the raw end under** by about 1 cm (½ in), matching where it was before. Look for the stitch holes or the faded line on the waistband.
3. **Clip the loop in place**, flat against the waistband, lined up with the bottom of the loop.
4. **Thread the needle** with about 60 cm (2 ft) of strong thread, doubled, and knot it.
5. **Start from inside the waistband**, so the knot is hidden, and come up through the folded loop.
6. **Sew straight across the loop**, back and forth, through all the layers, about 8 to 10 times, keeping the stitches close together. This is a hand bar tack.
7. **Wrap over the stitches.** Bring the needle up at one end of the bar and stitch closely over it from side to side, covering it like a satin stitch. This makes it much stronger.
8. **Finish off inside** with three small stitches on top of each other, and trim.
9. **Check the bottom** of the loop too. If it's loose, bar-tack that end the same way.

## If the waistband has torn

1. **Turn the jeans inside out** and cut a patch of denim or iron-on patch about 3 cm (1¼ in) bigger than the tear on each side.
2. **Iron or stitch it behind the tear**, then close the tear with small stitches through the patch.
3. **Sew the loop back on** through the waistband and the patch together, so the patch takes the strain.

## Repair options compared

| Damage | Fix | Time | What to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loop stitching broken | Hand bar tack | 10 minutes | Use strong thread and a thimble |
| Loop frayed at the end | Trim, fold, bar tack | 15 minutes | Loop sits a little shorter |
| Waistband torn | Patch inside, then bar tack | 30 minutes | The patch takes the strain |
| Loop missing | Make a new loop from denim | 40 minutes | Fold edges in, topstitch both sides |
| Lots of loops pulled | Alterations shop | — | A machine bar tack is stronger |

## Don't

- **Don't use one strand of thin thread.** A belt will snap it.
- **Don't sew only through the loop's edge.** Go through every layer.
- **Don't push the needle through thick denim without a thimble.**
- **Don't just glue it.** Fabric glue won't take the pull of a belt.

## Common questions

### Can you fix a belt loop without a sewing machine?
Yes. A hand bar tack made with strong thread, stitched back and forth 8 to 10 times and then wrapped, is plenty strong for a belt loop.

### What thread is best for jeans belt loops?
Jeans thread, upholstery thread or button thread. Doubled all-purpose polyester works if that's all you have. Gold or tan jeans thread matches most blue denim topstitching.

### Why do belt loops keep ripping?
Usually from pulling jeans up by the belt loops, or from a heavy belt with tools or a phone holder on it. Reinforcing the waistband behind the loop, and pulling jeans up by the waistband instead, both help.

### How do I make a new belt loop?
Cut a strip of denim about four times as wide as the finished loop and a few centimetres longer. Fold both long edges into the middle, fold in half again, and stitch along both sides. Then fold the ends under and bar-tack it on.

## The short version

Fold the loop's raw end under, clip it in place and sew straight across it 8 to 10 times with strong doubled thread, then wrap over those stitches to finish. If the waistband tore, patch it from the inside first.
