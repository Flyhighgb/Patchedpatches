You can iron, sew or glue a patch onto a hat, but the curve is what makes it tricky. A flat iron can't press evenly on a rounded front, so the patch lifts at the edges. The fix is to put something firm inside the hat to press against, use a small iron or press from both sides, and add a few stitches if the hat gets heavy wear. On beanies and synthetic caps, sew or glue instead of ironing.

Allow 15 to 30 minutes, plus drying time if you use glue.

## Check the hat first

Look at the label inside the hat, usually on the sweatband or a side seam.

- **Cotton, cotton twill or denim caps** usually take a hot iron. Iron-on patches work well here.
- **Polyester, nylon or acrylic** (many sports caps, beanies and bucket hats): if the iron symbol has one dot or is crossed out, don't use heat. Sew or glue.
- **Trucker caps** often have a foam front panel. Foam can melt or dent under an iron, so sew or glue.
- **Wool and felt hats**: check the label. Sewing is the safest choice.
- **No label?** Treat it as heat-sensitive and sew or glue.

Also check how stiff the front is. Most structured caps have a firm backing (buckram) behind the front panels. It holds the shape but makes it harder to push a needle through, and it may stop you reaching the inside.

## What you need

- The patch, and a way to see where it goes (a mirror, or put the hat on a ball or bowl).
- **For ironing:** a household iron or a [mini craft iron](amazon:mini craft iron), a thin cotton pressing cloth, and a rolled-up towel or a firm, rounded object to support the inside of the crown.
- **For sewing:** a strong needle (a curved needle helps on structured caps), polyester thread, a thimble and fabric clips.
- **For gluing:** a [washable textile glue](amazon:washable textile glue for patches).

## How to iron a patch onto a cap

1. **Find the position.** Put the hat on, or on a ball or bowl, and hold the patch in place. On a baseball cap the usual spot is centred on the front panels, just above the peak.
2. **Support the inside.** Stuff a rolled-up towel tightly into the crown so the front is firm. Pressing against nothing just squashes the hat.
3. **Heat the iron.** Use the hottest setting the label allows, with steam off. Briefly warm the area where the patch will go.
4. **Place the patch glue side down** and cover it with the pressing cloth.
5. **Press in small sections.** Use the tip or edge of the iron, rocking it gently over the curve. Hold firm, still pressure on each part for about 30 seconds, overlapping as you go. Pay extra attention to the edges.
6. **Press from inside if you can.** If the front isn't stiffened, reach inside with a mini iron and press the back of the fabric behind the patch.
7. **Let it cool completely** with the towel still inside, so the glue sets on the curve.

Follow the instructions that came with the patch if they differ. For more detail on heat and timing, see [how to iron on a patch](/guides/how-to-iron-on-a-patch/).

## How to sew a patch onto a cap

Sewing is the most secure way, and the only way for many beanies and synthetic caps.

1. **Hold the patch in place** with clips around the edge, or tack it with a dab of glue or a quick press if it's iron-on.
2. **Look inside.** If the sweatband is in the way, fold it down. On some caps you'll need to sew through it, which is fine as long as stitches are small.
3. **Thread about 60 cm (2 ft) of polyester thread** and knot the end.
4. **Start from inside** so the knot is hidden, coming up just inside the patch border.
5. **Whip stitch around the edge**, about 3 to 5 mm (⅛ to ¼ in) apart. On a stiff front, push the needle with a thimble. A curved needle lets you go in and out from the front if you can't reach behind.
6. **Finish inside** with a few small stitches on the spot and a knot.

On a **beanie**, keep a hand inside so you don't catch the back of the hat, and don't pull the thread tight. Knits need to stretch, so leave the stitches slightly loose. The full method is in our guide on [how to sew on a patch by hand](/guides/how-to-sew-on-a-patch-by-hand/).

## Gluing a patch onto a cap

Glue is handy for structured caps where you can't reach behind the front, and for foam trucker fronts. Spread a thin, even layer on the back of the patch, keeping a few millimetres from the edge so it doesn't squeeze out. Press it on, then hold it with clips or a tight elastic band around the crown while it dries. Most textile glues need a day or more to cure fully. Check the instructions, and ventilate the room if the glue is solvent-based.

## Which method suits which hat

| Hat | Best method | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| Cotton baseball cap | Iron on, then a few stitches | Support the inside with a towel |
| Structured cap with stiff front | Glue, or sew with a curved needle | Hard to reach behind the front panel |
| Trucker cap (foam front) | Sew or glue | Foam can melt under an iron |
| Beanie (knit) | Sew, loosely | Most are acrylic; heat can damage them |
| Bucket hat | Iron if cotton; sew if synthetic | Check the label |
| Wool or felt hat | Sew | Heat and glue can mark the fabric |

## Don't

- **Don't iron a flat iron straight onto an unsupported crown.** The patch won't bond and the hat may crease.
- **Don't iron PVC or rubber patches.** Sew them on using the stitching channel, or use a suitable glue.
- **Don't use steam.** It stops the glue bonding.
- **Don't put a patched cap in the washing machine.** Spot clean or hand wash, and don't tumble dry. Our guide to [washing clothes with patches](/guides/how-to-wash-clothes-with-patches/) has more.

## Common questions

### Can you iron a patch onto a hat?
Yes, if the hat is cotton or another fabric that takes a hot iron. Stuff the crown firmly with a towel, use a pressing cloth, and press in small sections. On polyester, acrylic or foam, sew or glue instead.

### Can I use a hair straightener to put a patch on a cap?
On an unstructured cap, a hair straightener can press the front from both sides at once. Use a cloth to protect the patch and press for a similar time to an iron. It won't fit around a stiff, structured front.

### How do you sew a patch onto a hat without going through the sweatband?
Fold the sweatband down and stitch through the front panel only. If it's sewn too close to reach behind, use a curved needle from the front, or glue the patch instead.

### Will a patch stay on a beanie?
Yes, if you sew it. Use loose stitches so the knit can still stretch. Iron-on glue doesn't grip knits well and many beanies can't take the heat.

## The short version

Check the label: iron-on works on cotton caps if you stuff the crown firmly and press in sections. Beanies, foam trucker fronts and synthetic caps should be sewn or glued. For anything worn a lot, add a few stitches around the edge.
