Most broken zips aren't broken at all. A zip that's **stuck** usually has fabric or dirt caught in it and needs easing free with a little lubricant. A zip that **splits open** behind the slider almost always has a slider that has worn loose, and a gentle squeeze with pliers closes it up again. Only when teeth are missing near the middle, or the tape is torn, does the whole zip need replacing.

## Work out what's wrong first

Zip the zip up and down slowly and look closely at what happens.

- **It won't move**, or only moves with force: something is caught in the slider, or the teeth are dirty or dry.
- **It closes, then the teeth open up again behind the slider**: the slider has worn and spread apart.
- **The pull tab has snapped off**: the slider is fine, it just needs a new pull.
- **One or two teeth are missing or bent**: at the very bottom you can sometimes work around it; anywhere else the zip will keep splitting there.
- **The slider has come off one side**: you can usually put it back on if the zip is open-ended (like a coat) or by removing the bottom stop.

## What you need

- **Small pliers**, ideally flat or needle-nose.
- **A graphite pencil, a bar of soap, a candle or a zip lubricant** for stuck zips.
- **Tweezers** for pulling out caught threads.
- **A replacement slider or a zip repair kit** if the old slider is badly worn. [Zip repair kits](amazon:zip repair kit replacement sliders) come with sliders in common sizes. The size is usually stamped on the back of the slider (for example 5 or 8).
- **A key ring, paper clip or cord** as a stand-in pull tab.

## How to fix a stuck zip

1. **Look for caught fabric.** Lining and loose threads are the usual culprits. Pull the fabric gently away from the slider while easing the slider backwards, not forwards.
2. **Pick out threads** with tweezers if they're wound into the teeth.
3. **Lubricate the teeth** near the slider. Rub a graphite pencil or the edge of a bar of soap or candle along both rows of teeth, on both sides.
4. **Work the slider** back and forth in short movements until it runs freely, then zip it fully up and down a few times.
5. **Clean off any excess** with a damp cloth so it doesn't mark the fabric.

## How to fix a zip that splits behind the slider

1. **Move the slider to the bottom** of the zip, below the teeth if you can.
2. **Look at the slider from the side.** You'll see a top plate and a bottom plate. When they've spread apart, the teeth don't lock as they pass through.
3. **Squeeze the plates very gently** with pliers, at the back end of the slider (the end the closed zip comes out of), a little on one side, then the other.
4. **Test it.** Zip it up. If it still splits, squeeze a tiny bit more. Too much and the slider won't move at all, so go slowly.
5. **Replace the slider** if squeezing doesn't help or the slider cracks. With a repair kit, remove the top stop with pliers, take off the old slider, thread on the new one the same way round, and crimp on a new stop (or sew a few tight stitches across the teeth as a stop).

## Zip problems and fixes

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix | Replace the zip? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Won't move | Fabric caught, dirty or dry teeth | Ease fabric out, lubricate | No |
| Splits behind slider | Worn, spread slider | Squeeze slider or fit a new one | Rarely |
| Pull tab snapped | Broken pull | Key ring, clip or a [clip-on zip pull](amazon:replacement zip pulls) | No |
| Slider off one side | Pulled past the stop | Refit slider, add a new stop | No |
| Missing teeth mid-way | Damaged teeth | Not repairable for long | Yes |
| Torn zip tape | Tape ripped from the teeth | Stitch the tear if small | Often |

## How to make a new stop

If the top stop is missing, the slider can come straight off. Thread a needle with strong thread and sew eight to ten tight stitches over the teeth just below the top on each side. That makes a stop the slider can't pass. Metal stops from a repair kit are neater on jeans.

## When to replace the zip

Replace the zip if teeth are missing in the middle, the tape is badly torn, or a new slider still won't close it. Replacing a trouser or skirt zip by hand is a manageable job; coat and jacket zips are long and fiddly, and an alterations shop is often the better call, especially for waterproofs.

## Don't

- **Don't force a stuck zip.** It bends teeth and tears tape.
- **Don't oil it with cooking oil or WD-40** on clothes. Both stain fabric.
- **Don't squeeze the slider hard all at once.** Small squeezes, test, repeat.
- **Don't iron over a plastic zip.** The teeth can melt.

## Common questions

### Why does my zip keep coming undone at the top?
On trousers and skirts, the zip may be fine and the fly lock (the small catch that stops the pull dropping) may be worn. Some sliders have no lock at all. Hook the pull up flat against the teeth, or fit a new locking slider from a repair kit.

### Can you fix a zip without pliers?
For a stuck zip, yes: soap, a candle or a pencil and patience usually do it. A splitting zip needs the slider squeezed, which is hard to do evenly without pliers.

### How do I know what size slider to buy?
Look on the back of the old slider for a number, such as 3, 5 or 8. It's the width of the closed teeth in millimetres, roughly. Match the type too: metal, moulded plastic or coil (nylon) zips each take their own sliders.

### Is it worth fixing a zip on a cheap jacket?
Usually yes. A new slider costs very little and takes minutes to fit, and it keeps the jacket wearable. Full zip replacement is more work, so weigh that against the jacket.

## The short version

Stuck zips need caught fabric eased out and the teeth lubricated with soap or pencil. Zips that split behind the slider need the slider gently squeezed with pliers, or a new slider. Only missing teeth or torn tape mean the zip itself needs replacing.
